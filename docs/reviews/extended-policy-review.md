## Independently verified support evidence

Alberta Education Annual Report 2024–25, printed page 62, identifies 2024–25 allocations of $116 million for the English as an Additional Language and Francisation grants supporting 95,737 children or students requiring additional English or French language support; and $57.5 million for the Refugee Student Grant supporting 11,137 refugee students. These are allocation and support-population descriptions, not evidence of service intensity, delivered learning gains or unique immigrant arrivals. Recipient groups may overlap. Do not sum them as unique students or equate language-support recipients with migrants.

The arithmetic averages are approximately $1,212 and $5,163 per reported recipient, respectively, but are not verified grant rates, total support expenditures or cost-effectiveness estimates. Differences in eligibility, support types and other funding make comparisons of the two averages unsuitable for ranking students or services. The concise report should prefer the original allocation totals and counts.

The report, printed page 66, describes a funding-model change for 2025–26: 30% current-year enrolment and 70% upcoming-year projections instead of a three-year average. This is evidence of a policy response to growth, not proof all funding lags disappeared. A recommendation for faster enrolment funding should acknowledge this existing mechanism and test forecast errors and midyear adjustments first.

## Migration interpretation gates

| Statistic | Defensible interpretation | Prohibited inference |
|---|---|---|
| All-age immigrant admissions | Demographic settlement context | Same number of new pupils or language-support recipients |
| Census immigrant stock | People classified as immigrants at the census reference date | Annual admissions or current enrolment |
| Non-permanent-resident stock | Estimated people in a defined status at a reference date | Gross new arrivals that quarter |
| Change in NPR stock | Net change including entries, exits and status changes | Gross inflow or new school admissions |
| Net interprovincial migration | Net population change from movement between provinces | International immigration or a direct EAL count |
| School-age migration | Closer demographic demand signal, if exact ages are supplied | School enrolment without residence, attendance and sector matching |
| EAL/Francisation grant recipients | Children/students eligible for language support | Immigrants, refugees, NPRs, or migrants uniquely counted |
| Refugee grant recipients | Supported students under the grant definition | All refugees resident in Alberta or unique extra language recipients |

No all-age migration series belongs in the denominator of spending per pupil. Net migration may miss substantial gross school entries and departures. Within-province moves and uneven local settlement can create capacity shortages without equivalent province-wide enrolment growth. Immigrant status does not identify language proficiency, interrupted schooling, disability, achievement or required support intensity. Measure those needs directly. Refugees, permanent residents, temporary residents and Canadian-born multilingual pupils are heterogeneous; avoid treating any status as an explanation of weak outcomes.

## Competing explanations and falsifiable checks

| Explanation | Necessary evidence or challenge |
|---|---|
| Reduced real resources constrained services | Actual authority expenditures, wage/input costs, vacancies, delivered support hours and class/space utilization; distinguish expenditure from allocations |
| Pandemic disrupted learning and assessment | Cohort exposure, participation, assessment weighting and local disruption history; test whether outcome changes persist in comparable later cohorts |
| Enrolment grew faster locally than staffing or spaces | Authority/school entries, exits and grade counts by term, hiring lead times and room utilization; provincial average alone is insufficient |
| Changing cohort composition altered aggregate results | Compare baseline achievement and student needs; report within-group progress as well as aggregate attainment; avoid blaming demographic categories |
| Assessment changed rather than learning | Stable test content/standards, writing eligibility, accommodations, language versions and participation; curriculum breaks can preclude comparisons |
| Housing, transport or mobility interrupted access | Attendance, school moves, transport reliability and assessed barriers; these mechanisms apply to many student populations |
| New allocations improved capacity but effects are delayed | Delivered staffing/support records and outcome lags; five-year completion cannot test an immediate annual funding effect |
| Implementation or allocation efficiency varies | Examine equal-need schools with different delivered services, leadership/staff retention and intervention fidelity; money is not a service measure |

Province-wide co-movement cannot discriminate among these explanations. More funding can respond to worsening needs, so a negative spending/outcome association can reflect targeting rather than harm. Demographic growth can affect total demand without demonstrating any effect on outcomes. Assessment participation can alter enrolment-based attainment independently of writer proficiency.

## Feasible decisions and metrics

1. **Ministry and school authorities:** compare projected and actual enrolment each term, including gross new registrations and withdrawals. Audit current funding-model forecast errors and timing of in-year adjustments before creating another funding mechanism. Track adjustment lead time and local unfunded pupil-months.
2. **Authorities:** conduct timely language and learning assessments for new and existing students according to need. Monitor median and upper-percentile time from enrolment/identified need to assessment and first appropriate support. Track delivered support hours, vacancies and interpreter access; spending and eligibility counts alone do not establish coverage.
3. **Authorities and capital planners:** combine school-level enrolment and housing-growth information with room utilization and transport constraints. Distinguish new seats from modernized/replacement seats; track occupancy and travel time rather than counting all capital projects as net capacity.
4. **Authorities:** pilot numeracy and language supports using baseline skill and assessed need. Where ethical and feasible, use a phased rollout or suitable comparison schools with pre-intervention trends. Track curriculum-aligned progress, attendance, support dose, staff retention and cost per improvement. Adjust for clustering and cohort changes; publish uncertainty, not just before/after percentages.
5. **Ministry:** check whether gains reach pupils with disabilities, Indigenous learners, multilingual pupils and pupils with interrupted schooling. Use relevant governance and privacy controls, suppress small cells and consult communities. Do not publish subgroup rankings that imply student identity causes performance.

A full-year pilot is an initial learning checkpoint, not an automatic causal verdict. Completion requires longer follow-up. Scale only when delivery fidelity, credible student gains, equity and workforce/space feasibility are established. Do not deny appropriate baseline support to create an evaluation control.

## Evaluation design challenge for implementation

A phased rollout is not automatically causal. If needier or faster-growing schools receive services first, compare baseline needs and pre-existing trends and justify assignment. A difference-in-differences design needs credible parallel pre-trends, stable measurement, limited spillovers and composition checks. Funding-formula changes are not automatically valid instruments: enrolment growth can directly affect classroom composition, crowding and outcomes. Report both the intended allocation and actual support received; vacancy and recruitment constraints can attenuate delivery.

Do not control away mechanisms when estimating the total funding effect: delivered support hours and class size can lie on the pathway from funding to outcomes. Use student needs and prior achievement for appropriate baseline adjustment, and separately analyze delivery mechanisms. Avoid a cost-per-improvement estimate based on uncontrolled aggregate changes. Include uncertainty, clustering at school/authority level, differential assessment participation and missing outcome follow-up.

A practical dashboard should separate: (a) demand—registered pupils and assessed needs; (b) capacity—vacancies, available instructional space and forecast error; (c) delivery—support hours and assessment-to-service waiting time; (d) outcomes—baseline-adjusted learning progress and completion; (e) cost—audited or recorded actual expenditure in the relevant program. This makes the response actionable without inferring every demand change is caused by migration or every outcome change is caused by funding.
