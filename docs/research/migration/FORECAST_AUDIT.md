# A reviewable enrolment and support forecast audit

The migration evidence supports testing **age-specific local forecasts**, not converting 13,624 net demographic migrants into a provincial school intake or teacher count. Start with the existing 30% current-year/70% upcoming-year projected-enrolment funding mechanism, described in Education's 2024–25 annual report, printed page 66. Verify exactly which forecast vintage and school-year counts each authority's grant uses before calculating errors.

## Preserve the forecasts that decision makers actually saw

For each authority and grade/ECS program, retain its budget-date forecast, later forecast revisions, count reference date, roster coverage and actual registration snapshots. A revised forecast produced after pupils arrive cannot be substituted for the original forecast in a backtest. Match the report's public/separate/francophone/charter boundary and Lloydminster exclusions, or explicitly label another boundary. Keep actual headcount, funded enrolment measures, classroom records and assessed service caseloads distinct.

At each September/December/term reference date, calculate:

- **Count error:** forecast minus actual pupils. Report the provincial signed sum and distribution across authorities/grades, so underprediction in one location is not hidden by overprediction elsewhere.
- **Weighted absolute percentage error:** sum of absolute count errors divided by the sum of actual counts for the same institutions/date. Also show errors in pupil counts; a provincial percentage alone can hide a severe local shortage.
- **Adjustment lead time:** days from a documented registration update or eligible funding request to approval and payment. Separate processing time, forecast revision time and the school-year/fiscal-year crossing.
- **Capacity exposure:** monthly pupils needing a seat or a specified support minus verified available seats or service capacity, where the result is positive. Report seat-months or unmet support-hours separately. Funding dollars are not automatically seats or service hours, and different needs must not be added into an invented common unit.

## Build the demographic bridge explicitly

Advance existing enrolled cohorts by grade, incorporating completion, retention, transitions between authorities/programs and exits. Model ECS and kindergarten entry from relevant local age cohorts, separately from older grades. Use local registrations and withdrawals to estimate the relationship between age-specific migration and school entry; do not assume that all migrants attend the report's institutions.

Use July–June international/interprovincial components as contextual priors, then align arrivals with the enrolment snapshot dates. The 2025–26 flow includes people arriving after a September or December 2025 count. Net international migration includes permanent-resident admissions, net emigration and NPR changes; status transitions need deduplication so an existing pupil becoming a permanent resident is not counted as a new school arrival. Within-Alberta moves also change school capacity and are absent from provincial net migration totals.

Forecast low/central/high enrolment scenarios using documented authority assumptions about arrival timing, school participation, residential location and grade entry. Show both a point estimate and a range, and backtest the ranges as well as their central estimates. Preserve Statistics Canada's population/migration vintage together: the September 2026 release revised NPR and population estimates from July 2021 onward, so a forecast-vintage audit must distinguish new information from actual pupil movement.

## Resource services from actual needs

For language support, track assessed English/French proficiency, time from registration to assessment, qualified staff availability, support hours delivered and progress. For refugee-related or other learning support, record the assessed service requirements and referral access separately. Language and refugee flags can overlap; immigrant status is not itself an assessed learning need. The archived 95,737 EAL/Francisation and 11,137 refugee-grant counts are useful program context, not an unduplicated new-arrival forecast.

Cost staff, rooms, transport and specified supports separately under each scenario using authority wage agreements, recruitment lead times and verified capacity. Compare allocations with actual delivered services. A pilot or phased rollout can then test whether additional capacity improves access and learning; provincial before/after migration and funding correlations cannot do that alone.
