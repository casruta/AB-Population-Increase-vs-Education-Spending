## What the verified numbers show

**All-age migration slowed, while migration in the school-age proxy remained positive.** In July 2025–June 2026, the immigrant component counted **50,158 permanent-resident admissions**. Subtracting **6,744 net emigration** and incorporating **−7,957 net non-permanent residents (NPRs)** gives **35,457 net international migration**. Add **21,662 net interprovincial migration** and **19,300 natural increase** to obtain **76,419 population growth**. This exactly matches the same-vintage July 1 stocks: **5,024,631 → 5,101,050**. These are preliminary demographic estimates, not an attribution of additional school pupils.

## The stronger connection to potential school demand

Tables [17-10-0014-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1710001401) and [17-10-0015-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1710001501) permit a single-age **5–17 demographic proxy**. Sum the thirteen individual ages, select **Total - gender**, and never sum overlapping age bands.

| July–June period | Age 5–17 immigrants | Net emigration | Net NPRs | Net international | Net interprovincial | Combined net migration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2021–22 | 8,444 | 1,336 | 3,249 | 10,357 | 1,072 | 11,429 |
| 2022–23 | 8,628 | 1,348 | 9,619 | 16,899 | 6,478 | 23,377 |
| 2023–24 | 9,702 | 1,254 | 14,562 | 23,010 | 6,438 | 29,448 |
| 2024–25 | 9,557 | 1,285 | 3,952 | 12,224 | 2,914 | 15,138 |
| 2025–26 | 8,374 | 1,303 | **3,408** | 10,479 | 3,145 | **13,624** |

For 2025–26, age 5–17 net NPRs are **positive**, despite the all-age decline. Overall migration totals therefore give insufficient information about likely school demand. Interprovincial movement also creates turnover: age 5–17 inflows were **9,845** and outflows **6,700**, indicating potential transition workload that the **3,145** net balance alone does not show.

**These are not 13,624 newly enrolled students.** Metadata defines age as **age at July 1** of the July–June period, not age on the migration date. September–August school years have different timing. Enrolment snapshots count students at a particular reference date, not a full-year pupil average: the July 2025–June 2026 flow cannot all be attributed to a September or December 2025 count. Later arrivals require in-year registrations or a later count. The proxy excludes children under five in Early Childhood Services (ECS) and students aged 18 or over; it includes residents outside the report's public/separate/francophone/charter boundary. It does not identify school attendance, private or home education, destination authority, language need, or repeat movements. NPR outflows can include a transition to permanent residence, offset by an immigrant entry, without physically leaving Alberta.

The preliminary 2025–26 interprovincial totals use Canada child benefit data; the preliminary age/gender distribution uses the average of three most recent tax-data years (Table 15 notes 9 and 12). Treat age-level figures as estimates, not an observed school intake register.

Age selection also changes the result: 2025–26 combined net migration is **12,299 at ages 6–17**, **15,446 at ages 5–18**, and **16,536 at ages 3–17**. None is a matched school denominator. Current [Table 17-10-0005-01](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=1710000501) resident age 5–17 population rose **799,473 → 806,506 (+7,033)** between July 2025 and July 2026. Migration is not identical to fixed-age population growth because cohorts age into and out of the band, with deaths and estimate adjustments also relevant. July 2025 ages 5–17 become ages 6–18 one year later, while the July 2026 ages 5–17 stock includes prior four-year-olds and excludes prior seventeen-year-olds. The migration proxy is classified by start-year age; subtracting 13,624 from 7,033 would therefore not estimate a valid ageing residual.

## Documented service needs

The archived [Education Annual Report 2024–25](https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/8b226e68-1227-4aec-87a5-b573f3bfb062/resource/dcf58041-1aef-4f46-9452-b6a58f29d856/download/educ-annual-report-2024-2025.pdf), **printed page 62**, reports:

- **$116 million allocated jointly to EAL and Francisation**, supporting **95,737 children or students requiring additional English or French language support**.
- **$57.5 million allocated through the Refugee Student Grant**, supporting **11,137 refugee students** with language, social and educational supports.

The first count is English **and French**, not EAL alone and not immigrant arrivals. The two groups may overlap and must not be added. Neither count measures all migrants' needs, nor implies every immigrant needs language support. Grant allocations do not establish services delivered, sufficiency, or audited expenditure per student. A percentage using the report's narrower matched enrolment would lack demonstrated matching coverage and is omitted.

The archived Fiscal Plan 2026, printed pages **35–36**, forecast 2026 census-year net NPRs **−30,000**, net interprovincial migration **24,000**, and population growth **about 55,000 (1.1%)**; 2027 net NPRs **−21,000**. These are forecasts of another vintage, not substitutes for the current actual preliminary estimates. Printed page **79** budgets **$1.8 billion** for several specialized supports jointly, including EAL/refugee programs; it does not provide a separable EAL cost or recipient forecast.

## Recommendations supported by the evidence

1. **Forecast by grade, authority and location.** Advance each existing enrolled cohort, model ECS entry and grade completion, then add age-specific international/interprovincial flows and enrolment participation assumptions. Translate July–June flows to September counts and allow in-year arrivals. Reconcile projections against monthly student registrations and exits, housing/school catchments and capacity.
2. **Use gross movements for transition workload and net movements for longer-run capacity.** Track newcomer registrations, exits, transfers and duplicate students in authority data; a small net change can coexist with substantial turnover. Status transitions should not be counted as a second arrival.
3. **Resource language and settlement services from assessed needs.** Budget qualified EAL/Francisation staff, interpreters, family orientation, social supports and referral access using assessed proficiency, actual caseload, support duration and arrival timing. Preserve separate, potentially overlapping refugee and language-support flags and monitor progress and access.
4. **Keep funding categories and denominators matched.** Use public/separate/francophone/charter ECS-to-grade-12 enrolment with the corresponding allocations or actual expenses; do not divide all-age immigration or broad ministry spending by pupils. Cost teacher/support hours, class capacity, transport and facilities under transparent low/central/high enrolment scenarios instead of treating a population ratio as a budget requirement.
5. **Evaluate delivery before claiming effects.** Connect actual staff and service access to matched pupil attendance and learning measures with credible comparisons and confounders. Aggregate migration, spending and outcomes cannot identify causality or blame a group for a provincial trend.
