# Observed Alberta K–12 outcomes and cost pressures

**Earlier archived checkpoint.** Newer verified assessment results and classroom data are in [the refreshed findings](../refresh-outcomes/FINDINGS.md). Retained here for the evidence trail.

Evidence extracted from the archived official **Education Annual Report 2024–2025** and independently checked by an outcome-verification subagent. See `provenance.json` and `outcomes.csv`. Printed page numbers refer to report pagination.

**Financial and outcome records show different-period trends; neither establishes what spending caused.** Cohorts, outcome horizons and observation windows differ. Province-level temporal comparisons do not control for COVID-19, changing assessment arrangements, enrolment composition, student needs, curriculum, local implementation or family circumstances.

## Definitions that must accompany interpretation

- Completion (pp. 22–23, 87): percentage completing within five years of entering Grade 10, with an estimated out-migration adjustment. Completion includes diploma/equivalency/certificate, Alberta post-secondary/apprenticeship entry or passing five specified Grade 12 courses. Some cognitive/severe multiple disability categories are excluded. This is broader than graduation with a diploma. Pandemic exam cancellations, optional participation and weighting changes may affect comparability.
- PAT achievement (pp. 27–31, 88–89): enrolment-weighted aggregate of language arts subjects and mathematics courses, including Knowledge and Employability. Acceptable standard includes excellence. Students absent, excused or with invalidated results have no achievement results; participation must accompany interpretation. Overall LA participation changed 84.2%→83.3%, and mathematics 83.9%→84.9%, in 2022–23→2023–24. Do not call these figures a writer-only pass rate or interpret the complement as confirmed failed writers.
- PAT missing periods: 2019–20 and 2020–21 unavailable; 2021–22 affected by pandemic participation and security-breach exclusions. Avoid a clean uninterrupted long-run PAT trend.
- Dropout (p. 24): students aged 14–18 in the prior year who neither completed nor enrolled the following year in K–12, Alberta post-secondary or apprenticeship. About 19% of 2022–23 dropouts resumed education in 2023–24. This is not an attendance measure.

## Service-pressure evidence

Annual report printed pp. 95–96 attributes the 2024–25 consolidated expense increase partly to enrolment growth and inflation: instruction increased $280.8 million; transportation $43.9 million; operations/maintenance $27 million. Ministry commentary cites teachers/instructional support, transport insurance/contractors, utilities and school insurance. These are financial cost drivers, **not evidence that class sizes increased, staffing fell or unmet needs rose**. The consolidated ministry/school-board scope differs from school-authority spending excluding amortization and should not be spliced into that series.

## Actionable next investigation

Prioritize a mathematics recovery pilot with baseline diagnostic assessment and attendance monitoring. Allocate support using school/authority need and enrolment growth; compare phased implementation against comparable schools and pre-existing trends. Track gains, subgroup equity, support hours and cost per improvement. Secure school-level linked spending, staffing FTE, attendance, assessed need and outcome records before claiming funding effects. Do not promise a causal payoff or rank interventions by return without evaluation.
