Ministry Fact Sheet – Mandate and Structure
The Ministry of Education and Childcare provides leadership, direction and oversight for an accessible, affordable
and high-quality childcare system and to enable the delivery of a student-centred education system that prepares
students for lifelong success.

Education and Childcare is responsible for a provincial Kindergarten to Grade 12 (K-12) curriculum that provides
the foundation for all learners to achieve provincial student learning outcomes and succeed in developing the
knowledge, skills and competencies needed to pursue their career pathways and make meaningful contributions to
their communities and the world. The ministry supports a range of education options to promote choice within the
education system, including public, separate, francophone, charter and independent schools as well as alternative
programs, early childhood services and home education programs.

Education and Childcare is implementing targeted actions to address class size and classroom complexity. These
actions include creating new and renovated student spaces across the province to keep pace with Alberta’s growing
student population, supporting the hiring of new teachers and educational assistants and providing school
authorities with access to specialized learning supports. The Class Size and Complexity Cabinet Committee will use
data collected from the education system to allocate strategic investments to address classroom complexity.
Mandatory literacy and numeracy screenings will strengthen early identification of foundational learning needs and
enable evidence-informed planning for interventions.

Education and Childcare collaborates with Canadian and international jurisdictions and organizations to share
research and best practices that keep Alberta students competitive on the global stage. The ministry collaborates
with education and ministry partners to support Alberta’s diverse students. The department works to ensure eligible
students have access to francophone education while encouraging opportunities for French education through
French language programs. The department also supports the education system to improve outcomes for First
Nations, Métis and Inuit students, incorporating Indigenous histories, cultures, languages and perspectives in
curriculum and promoting foundational knowledge for teachers and school leaders.

Education and Childcare is committed to achieving a sustainable childcare system that benefits Alberta families and
supports childcare providers. The ministry works to increase access to childcare by supporting childcare providers
in offering high quality, accessible, affordable and inclusive childcare through licensing, funding and monitoring
childcare programs to ensure the safety and well-being of children.

Outcome 1
Alberta’s students are successful

The ministry supports the success of Alberta’s students through a strong Kindergarten to Grade 12 education
system that provides opportunities for students to build the foundations for successful and fulfilling lives.

Key Objectives
1.1 Provide curriculum that focuses on literacy, numeracy, financial literacy, citizenship and practical life skills.
1.2 Collaborate with other ministries to expand mental health and well-being supports for students.
1.3 Develop and maintain comprehensive provincial assessments, including mandatory literacy and numeracy
    screenings, that assess progress, identify learning gaps and monitor student success.

Initiatives Supporting Key Objectives
•    A total allocation of $10.8 billion in operating expense in 2026-27 to support instruction and early childhood
     services programs in school authorities.
•    A total of $1.84 billion is allocated in 2026-27 for learning support funding to attend to unique and diverse
     learning needs of children and students, including supporting student mental health and well-being in schools.

Outcome 2
First Nations, Métis and Inuit students in Alberta are successful

Alberta Education and Childcare collaborates with education partners and provides supports to improve First
Nations, Métis and Inuit student education outcomes.

Key Objectives
2.1 Strengthen education programs, policies and initiatives for First Nations, Métis and Inuit students’ success.
2.2 Increase understanding of approaches to First Nations, Métis and Inuit student achievement and promote
    effective practices in Indigenous Education.
2.3 Enhance relationship-building with First Nations, Métis and Inuit communities, partners and schools to
    enhance student success and advance reconciliation.

Initiatives Supporting Key Objectives
•    A total of $4.6 million is allocated in 2026-27 to address the unique learning needs of First Nations students,
     including specialized learning supports and both universal and targeted mental health supports and services.

Performance Metrics
2.a Performance Measure: High school completion rate of self-identified First Nations, Métis and Inuit
    students (2024-25 data available June 2026)
     High school completion is important for entry into the labour force and post-secondary programs. In 2023-24,
     69.7 per cent of self-identified First Nations, Métis and Inuit students completed high school within five years
     of entering Grade 10.

Outcome 3
Alberta’s students have access to a variety of learning opportunities to enhance competitiveness in the
modern economy

The ministry provides innovative learning opportunities to ensure a variety of career pathways for students to enter
post-secondary education or their chosen fields in the workforce.

Key Objectives
3.1 Enhance career education programming opportunities that enable grades 7 to 12 students to explore
    various occupations.
3.2 Collaborate with other ministries, education and industry partners to improve student preparedness
    in transitioning from high school into a variety of careers including high demand trades and
    post-secondary training.
3.3 Support collegiate school opportunities to create more pathways for students to post-secondary education and
    the workforce.

Initiatives Supporting Key Objectives
•    $6.6 million is allocated to school authorities to provide sustainable, predictable career education funding.
•    $32.1 million is allocated to collegiate schools, in support of increased access to career education programming.

Outcome 4
Alberta’s K-12 education system and workforce are well-managed

Alberta Education and Childcare supports the professional growth and development of teachers, school leaders,
school authority leaders and educational assistants. The department directs the education system and oversees the
administration of education programs and services through legislation, provision of funds and planning suitable
learning facilities.

Key Objectives
4.1 Implement school capital strategies to renovate and increase the number of student spaces in the communities
    of greatest need.
4.2 Establish a data-driven approach to monitor and allocate resources towards mitigating the impact of class size
    and classroom complexity on students and teachers.
4.3 Develop and implement abbreviated teacher certification pathways for qualified individuals with degrees,
    diplomas or trade credentials in high-demand fields to teach in Alberta’s K-12 classrooms.
4.4 Continue to create operational and financial efficiencies within the education system by identifying options
    that improve consistency and streamline processes and procedures.
4.5 Promote choice in education to enhance student learning through a variety of schooling options.

Initiatives Supporting Key Objectives
•      $4.1 billion in capital investment is allocated over three years to maintain, enhance, plan and build
       new schools.
•      $355 million is allocated in 2026-27 to address classroom complexity.

Performance Metrics

       Attracting and retaining excellent teachers enables high learning outcomes and greater student achievement. In
       2024-25, 70.3 per cent of principals agreed that they are able to attract and retain teachers in their schools.

Outcome 5
Alberta families have access to high quality, inclusive, and affordable childcare options

The ministry is committed to providing a high quality, affordable and sustainable childcare system to meet the
needs of Alberta families.

Key Objectives
5.1 Support access to affordable, high-quality and inclusive licensed childcare by negotiating a financially
    sustainable federal childcare agreement.
5.2 Enhance quality licensed childcare through workforce investments that strengthen the recruitment and
    retention of skilled early childhood educators in the childcare system.
5.3 Improve access to affordable out of school care programs for families, through income-tested family supports.

Initiatives Supporting Key Objectives
•    In 2026-27, the ministry will allocate $1.77 billion for affordable, high-quality and inclusive licensed childcare.
•    In 2026-27, the ministry will allocate $344 million to support the recruitment and retention of early childhood
     educators working in licenced childcare facilities.
