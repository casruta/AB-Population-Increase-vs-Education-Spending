## Completion remains lagged, with substantial cohort differences

The annual report2024–25, latest Fall 2025 provincial assurance report and
2026–29 business-plan backgrounder all report completion observations ending
**2023–24**. Five-year completion86.2% in 2019–20→87.1% in 2023–24 (+0.9 pp)
is below its2021–22 peak 88.6% (−1.5 pp). Three-year completion 80.4% in 2022–23
→81.4% in 2023–24 improves1.0 pp, while the five-year rate declines1.0 pp;
these concern different Grade 10 entering cohorts and cannot be combined.

For the same Grade 10 entering cohort2019–20, provincial completion was 80.7%
within three years (2021–22) and 87.1% within five (2023–24):6.4 pp additional
completion. Self-identified First Nations, Métis and Inuit students had 57.0%
and 69.7%, respectively:12.7 pp additional completion (annual pp23,40–41).
The2023–24 five-year difference between overall and self-identified Indigenous
results is 17.4 pp. The overall group includes Indigenous students, so it is not a
non-Indigenous comparison. Self-identification, cohort membership, disability
exclusions, migration adjustments and pandemic exam rules matter. These results
support evaluating sustained pathways and re-engagement supports with communities;
they do not establish the cause of group differences.

## Current classroom snapshot, with explicit coverage limits

Official `class-size-2025-2026.xlsx` contains self-reported Alberta Classroom
Insights Portal data **as of 24November 2025**, released in the catalogue
February 2026. Across included **Grades 7–9 class records**, **7,642 of35,313
(21.64%) exceed 30 pupils**. The denominator includes 914 suppressed records;
metadata says suppression applies below 10, so these cannot exceed 30.
`analyze_classes.py` reproduces all calculations without third-party packages.

The workbook covers 61 public/separate/francophone authorities and 1,560 schools.
It excludes alternative programs, outreach/online and special-purpose schools,
special-education subjects, invalid grade ranges and reported enrolment outside
1–60. A classroom is a teacher/student-group instructional portion: pupils can
appear in multiple classes. Consequently 21.64% describes included class records,
**not unique students**, and cannot be generalised to all Alberta classes.
Unweighted means calculated from disclosed records omit smaller suppressed
classes and should not be headline estimates. Older class-size sources use a
different collection regime; no definition-matched increase was verified.

## Staffing and support perceptions, not staffing counts

Annual pp68–69 give principals' average agreement across two attracting and
retaining items:74.5% in 2023–24→70.3% in 2024–25. The ministry describes the
year-to-year result as statistically stable despite the numerical decline.
The principal survey2024–25 sample is359, response rate35.5%, reported full
sample margin±4.7 pp (p81); nonresponse remains relevant.

Annual pp76–77 give the overall support-access agreement measure73.9%→72.9%.
It averages unrounded respondent-group means with equal group weights, across
high-school students, parents, teachers and trustees, and averages different
items within groups (p82). It is not a pupil-weighted service-access rate.
The report calls the year-to-year measure stable. Teacher surveys switched
telephone→online in 2021–22, limiting earlier comparisons. Reported dissatisfaction
can guide investigation, while service waiting times and actual staffing FTE still
need measurement. Learning-space satisfaction75.5%→74.2% has similar limitations
(pp78–79).

Budget 2026 Fiscal Plan p79 budgets$355million for class size/complexity in2026–27
and$1.4billion over 2026–27 to2028–29, supporting 3,000 teachers and 1,500
education assistants over three years. These are prospective funding/hiring
commitments, not verified actual hires. The PDF is internally dated 26February 2026;
that agrees with the declared vintage. Live financial verification is the separate
finance review's responsibility.

## Targeted evaluation warranted by the evidence

Use the Grades 7–9 mathematics decline and reported classroom distribution to
identify schools needing diagnostic assessment, additional teaching/support time
or smaller instructional groups. Combine school/authority data on need,
attendance, recent arrival, language support, staff vacancies, services and
spending. Do not select solely on high enrolment or migration status.
Pilot phased allocation with baseline and follow-up mathematics assessments,
participation, actual delivered support hours, waiting times and per-pupil costs;
compare changes against comparable schools and existing trends. Diagnose
recruitment feasibility before promising new staffing capacity. Completion and
re-engagement require longer follow-up and community-designed support. Aggregate
funding, migration and outcomes remain associations, with no causal effect identified.

## Independent numerator and writer-denominator cross-check

The live PAT page links a provincial multiyear table with observations through
2024–25, generated 26 September 2025 and current as of 15 September 2025.
Unlike the catalogue's historical multiyear series, this active webpage source
contains fresh individual-course counts. It lists 2026–27 curriculum bulletins
but still labels the latest result tables 2021–2025.

Summing regular Mathematics 9 and Knowledge and Employability Mathematics 9
course-entry counts reproduces the update's rounded attainment and participation
figures. Writer-entry attainment also declines, from 62.06% to 60.57% in
2023–24 → 2024–25. This is a derived complementary measure rather than the
ministry's published enrolment-denominator headline. It does not hold student
composition fixed or establish an effect of spending, migration or class size.

For 2024–25, the enrolled-entry distribution is 51.66% achieving acceptable
standard, 33.62% writing below standard and 14.72% with results unavailable.
The complement of 51.7% is therefore not the share of writers failing.
`math-numerator-verification.json` records counts, calculations, exact source
URL, hash and PDF pages 17 and 20. Source notes also identify wildfire-related
participation disruption in 2023–24 as well as 2022–23.
